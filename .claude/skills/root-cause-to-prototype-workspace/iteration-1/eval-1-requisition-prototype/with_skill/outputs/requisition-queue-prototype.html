Requisition queue: one lane vs. two

Sketch of a fix for the complaint that a cracked vest plate requisition sat 11 days waiting on a quartermaster signature behind routine orders like boot laces. Toggle between the current design and a proposed safety-tiered queue below.

Evidence note: this is built from a single interview aside (Halloran, 5 Sept 2026) plus the Supply process one-pager — there is no ticket queue or timing data for requisitions in this project, unlike the rich multi-source dispatch data. Treat this as a hypothesis to react to, not a validated fix. See report.md for the full gap list.
Current: single queue
Proposed: safety-tiered queue

Quartermaster queue — today

One FIFO queue. A priority field exists on the requisition form, but nothing in the documented flow sorts or routes on it — it's informational only. Everything waits its turn in submission order.

The "priority" field Halloran set on his requisition doesn't appear anywhere in this ordering — matching his account that it "doesn't seem to change anything about how fast it actually moves."

Quartermaster queue — with a safety-critical lane

Requisitions tagged against a short list of safety-critical equipment categories (armor/plate, harness/fall-protection, breathing apparatus) jump to a dedicated lane with a target SLA. Everything else keeps the existing FIFO queue untouched — no change to the bulk of low-stakes requests.

The vest plate is pulled into a dedicated safety-critical lane with its own 48-hour target, visible to the quartermaster as a badge rather than a silent form field. Standard-lane wait times are unchanged — this doesn't ask the quartermaster to work faster overall, only to see urgency instead of inferring it.
