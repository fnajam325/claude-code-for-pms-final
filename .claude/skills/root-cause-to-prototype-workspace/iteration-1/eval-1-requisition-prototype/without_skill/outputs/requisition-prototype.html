What changed, mechanically

1. Priority field now drives sort order and SLA, not just a label — safety-critical requests sit in their own lane, above everything else, regardless of filing date.
2. Each lane carries a clock. When a request's SLA is breached, it's visually flagged (as shown above) instead of silently aging.
3. Auto-escalation: a safety-critical request with no quartermaster action inside 24 hours routes itself to a backup approver automatically — it no longer depends on someone noticing.
4. Single-signer bottleneck removed for the top lane only — routine and operational approvals still go through the primary quartermaster, keeping the change small and low-risk.

Root cause summary

Why the vest plate sat for 11 days

1. One undifferentiated queue. Rook Supply's one-pager confirms the core flow is: handler raises a requisition → it routes to a quartermaster for approval → fulfillment is tracked. There is no tiering by urgency in that flow.

2. The priority field is cosmetic. Halloran set it ("there's a priority field, I set it") and it had no effect on queue position — confirmed directly in the interview transcript (5 Sept 2026).

3. No SLA or escalation path. Nothing in the product surfaces when a request has gone past a reasonable review window, and nothing reroutes it to another approver if the assigned quartermaster is slow, out, or overloaded. The request simply waits for one person to act.

4. It's already a known gap, not a surprise. The Q3 2026 roadmap lists "Requisition approval chains" (Supply, target 4.3) as a committed item owned by Product — this investigation adds the concrete failure case and a minimal, shippable scope to point that committed work at.
