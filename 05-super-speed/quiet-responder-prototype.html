How the card decides

  • It shows after 3 days with no offers, or when a responder has under a quarter of their usual offers in 7 days. At most once a week.
  • It uses only offer history and availability. No location.
  • Hiding the card does not end the watch. The chip stays in the header. Click the chip to bring the card back.
  • The watch ends only when the responder takes an offer. An offer that goes unanswered does not end it.
  • Every card shows its last 7 days against the usual, with no alert, so a slow decline or a heavy week is visible.

What this shows, and what it doesn't

  • Shows promises 1 and 2: the handler card and the responder message. Both go out together.
  • The check-in wording and the replies are proposals. "Your availability and tags look right from here" must be something Rook can really check.
  • Still open before this ships: "we're looking into why" needs a named owner and a date, or it should go. So do the "[time to be agreed]" update windows.
  • Still open: a flat 3-day rule fires on low-volume responders. It should scale to each responder's own gaps. Ravi to test on the pre-4.2 data.
  • Still open: whether Rook can show that an offer reached the phone. Nothing here does.
  • A dated "on leave" status is a separate item, not part of this check-in.
  • Does not show the way back (promise 3), the options for the four, or the stop rules. Those are still held.
  • These are example screens. No real system changes.
