THE SETUP

Release 4.2 quietly locked a handful of responders out of the job rotation almost entirely, while making everyone else's offers vanish faster than they could react — one routing change, two symptoms.

Four responders went from ~12 offers a week to 0–1 and never recovered, because the scoring code has no way for a low score to climb back up on its own. Nobody shipped a bug. They shipped a trap with no exit.

The Story, Issue By Issue

ISSUE #1 — ORIGIN STORY

Meeting Rook, building the map

Built the working-context file from the full new-hire packet, team directory, and handoff doc — and confirmed it covered the job without drowning the next reader in it.

THE EXACT PROMPT:

"is there too much information in the claude.md currently? do we need to consolidate at all at this point?"

ISSUE #2 — SUPER HEARING

Two piles of evidence, one disagreement

Four interviews and 25 tickets, read in full and cross-checked against each other — 3 of 4 handlers volunteered the vanishing-callout complaint unprompted, in a call that was never about routing.

THE EXACT PROMPT:

"Read everything in 00-rook/feedback/interviews/. These are four conversations with the people who run the console for our responders. Tell me what they're unhappy about, group it, tell me how many of the four raised each thing, and quote one line for each so I can hear how they actually said it."

ISSUE #3 — REWIND

Five agents, one corrected theory

A parallel 5-agent investigation into the raw ping data confirmed the collapse was real and structural — then falsified my own working theory: Meteor Mite had the worst acceptance rate on the whole roster, not a great one, so "strong history offsets distance" wasn't the real pattern.

THE EXACT PROMPT:

"i want you to administer 5 agents, one to explore each area here. i want these agents to debate each other on their findings, and then align on where they feel is the root-cause to all of this. if they cannot align on that, at least help me understand what information they need to better understand how we solve this?"

ISSUE #4 — X-RAY VISION

The exact line of code

Found the one place points come off (a decline or a timeout, same penalty) and the one place they go back on (a yes, and nothing else) — with a 2019 TODO admitting the score never recovers on its own. Sent the confirmed answer to Marcus's unanswered Slack question straight to the team channel.

THE EXACT PROMPT:

"Find me the part of this code that takes points off somebody when they miss a ping or turn one down. Show it to me and explain it in plain English. Then find me every single thing in this code that puts points back on."

ISSUE #5 — SUPER SPEED

Building the fix, testing it on heroes

Built the Near-Miss Grace Window prototype and put it in front of simulated responders for honest, unbiased feedback — twice — adding a missed-offer reason check-in, a security-gated distance line, and a mission-board view from what they said.

THE EXACT PROMPT:

"lets start with near-miss grace window. i want you play the role of these super heroes are having friction with responding to alerts. show them the prototype, ask them unbiased questions that will otherwise help us understand how effective this capability will be. once you're done, come back to me, give me their feedback, and lets determine what improvements we should make?"

ISSUE #6 — SIDEKICKS

A sidekick that keeps this from happening quietly again

Built the review-checklist skill — it checks any brief for a named owner, checkable success criteria, consistent scope, and problem-before-fix ordering — then scheduled it to run every Monday against the latest brief, automatically, with the results committed back into the repo.

THE EXACT PROMPT:

"Make a skill called review-checklist. When I point it at a brief, it checks that the brief: names who owns it, says how we'll know it worked, keeps the same scope start to finish, and explains the problem before the fix."

Our Hero Strikes Back: The Fix

The Near-Miss Grace Window: a missed offer inside a short grace period gets logged as a near miss instead of a flat no, with an honest reason check-in and a visible promise that scoring doesn't silently change underneath it. Reskinned once into a bold, full-color hero version of the same mechanic.

Next Issue: How This Doesn't Happen Again

Two sidekicks are on permanent duty now, both living in .claude/skills/ in this repo, not just in one conversation:

root-cause-to-prototype — the investigation framework itself, turned into a skill: gather every source completely, cross-reference on purpose, write falsifiable hypotheses, simulate before building, and never call a theory confirmed without naming what's still unverified.

review-checklist — a fast gate on any brief before it ships, now running on autopilot: every Monday at 9am, it checks whatever brief is current for a named owner, a real success metric, consistent scope, and problem-before-fix order, and writes the result straight into this repo's history — win or lose, every week, automatically.
