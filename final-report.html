THE SETUP

What happened with Release 4.2, plainly

1 · What shipped, 12 Aug

  • Closeness counts more in who gets asked first (0.45 → 0.60). How often they said yes lately counts less (0.40 → 0.25).
  • Each offer waits 60 seconds, not 90.
  • The console remembers filters, plus three defect fixes.

Source: config.py, release notes

2 · Two things went wrong at once

A. Four responders went from about 12 offers a week to 0–1 and never came back.

B. Everyone else's offers vanish faster than they can answer. Tickets ran about 3× normal.

Source: callout-history.csv, tickets, Slack export

3 · Why the four never came back

Every offer that doesn't end in a yes costs 0.12 points. A no and a missed offer cost the same. Only a yes gives back 0.08.

So a responder needs about 6 yes in 10 just to hold still, and the only way up is to be offered a job.

Source: history.py, config.py

Still not known: whether that scoring was meant to work that way (Wen hasn't answered), whether missed offers are slowness or offers that never reach the phone, and whether scores survive a restart. The code shows the mechanism. It doesn't show the intent. (Open questions in the Helen brief, section 7.)

ISSUE #5 · SUPER SPEED · MODULE 5

A prototype scoped by nine questions

Before any building, Claude asked clarifying questions one at a time, and the answers fixed the scope: two promises, a scripted walkthrough, one page showing two devices. The result is the clickable prototype on the next slide.

What got built

  • Promise 1: a card on the handler's console when a responder gets no offers for 3 days.
  • Promise 2: a message on the responder's phone, so nobody quietly disappears.
  • Held: promise 3, a way back. It waits for Wen's answers.

Two rounds of simulated user tests (8, then 9 testers) changed it twice. Round one made "Dismiss" into "Hide for now". Round two found the check-in blamed the responder's phone, so it was rewritten for v3.

NEXT ISSUE

How my skill keeps this from happening quietly again

The review-checklist skill runs four checks on any brief. Here is what 4.2 looked like through each one.

1 · OwnerWhat 4.2 looked like: Marcus asked on 14 Aug whether the new weights applied to responders who'd been declining. No answer is on record. Nobody has confirmed with Helen which Q3 items are still committed.The skill asks for: a named person, not a team, who decides.
2 · SuccessWhat 4.2 looked like: what success looked like for the routing change is still an open question to Helen and Marcus.The skill asks for: a measure, a target and a time, so a change can fail.
3 · ScopeWhat 4.2 looked like: the weights and the timeout shipped together on one day, so the data can't separate their effects.The skill asks for: one scope start to finish, with adjacent changes named and set aside.
4 · Problem firstWhat 4.2 looked like: the reason for the 60-second timer isn't documented, and the previous PM says she "made calls faster than I checked them".The skill asks for: what's broken, for whom and at what cost, before the fix.

It runs weekly

Scheduled for Monday mornings. Its first run flagged 4 of 4 briefs. Later runs are logged in 05-super-speed/review-checklist-log.md.

It improves the briefs

On the Helen brief, the 12 Oct run failed scope and the 26 Oct run passed all four. The brief was rewritten as v4 in between.

It has a front door

A second skill, interview-to-build, asks until Claude is 95% sure what you want before it builds. It produced the On leave PRD in 9 questions.

What it can't do: the skill is a gate on briefs. It would not have found the scoring trap on its own. It makes the missing owner, measure, scope and problem visible before something ships.
